## Project Overview
This project investigates the evolution of English-language song lyrics from the 1950s to the 2020s through the lens of text mining. Using a large corpus of lyrics spanning seven decades, we applied a suite of natural language processing techniques—including sentiment analysis, frequency-based exploration, bigram networks, and topic modeling—to understand how the emotional tone, linguistic structure, and thematic focus of popular music have changed over time.

The aim was not only descriptive but also interpretive: to connect lyrical patterns to broader cultural, musical, and social transformations. The final analysis provides insights into how popular music reflects and amplifies shifts in collective mood, identity, and modes of artistic expression.

## Objectives

- **Text Preprocessing**: Cleaning and normalizing raw textual data.
- **Exploratory Text Analysis**: Investigating term frequency, word associations, and document structure.
- **Topic Modeling**: Uncovering latent themes in the corpus using unsupervised learning methods such as LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation).
- **Sentiment Analysis**: Measuring emotional tone using lexicon-based techniques.
- **Visualization**: Communicating findings through interpretable plots and word clouds.

## Key Findings

Our analysis reveals a profound and multidimensional evolution in song lyrics across the decades:

- Linguistic Transformation: Lyrics have shifted from the romantic and idealized tone of the 1950s and 60s toward a lexicon that is more direct, raw, and often explicit—mirroring the rise of genres like hip-hop, alternative, and trap.
- Emotional Shift: Sentiment analysis shows a clear decline in lyrical optimism and a growing presence of negative or intense emotions. While joy remains, themes of sadness, fear, and anger have steadily increased, especially since the 1990s.
- Thematic Diversification: Topic modeling revealed two major thematic groupings:
    - Emotionally introspective themes (e.g., love, pain, identity)
    - Externally oriented themes (e.g., partying, fame, materialism): The former has grown in prevalence, suggesting a trend toward personal storytelling and emotional authenticity.
- Complexity and Maturity: Lyrics have become longer and more nuanced, often addressing personal and societal struggles. This reflects a maturation in songwriting that parallels broader cultural openness to vulnerability and complexity.
- Cultural Resonance: Ultimately, song lyrics have evolved as mirrors of their time—growing more diverse, emotionally charged, and reflective of social change. They show how music continues to serve not just as entertainment, but as a vehicle for identity, resistance, and connection.
